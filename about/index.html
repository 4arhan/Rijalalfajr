How it works

Arrive for Fajr. Scan. Watch your mosque rise.

1

The mosque puts up a code

An admin registers the mosque and opens the mosque screen on a tablet or TV by the entrance. It shows a QR code that changes every 30 seconds, so a forwarded screenshot is useless.

2

You check in at dawn

Point your phone camera at the code. Check-in opens shortly before the adhan and closes at sunrise, worked out from the mosque's own location. Children are ticked on the attendance list or show a printed card.

3

The streak and the podium do the rest

Every dawn adds to your streak and your place on your mosque's leaderboard. Every check-in also counts for the mosque on the world leaderboard.

Features

Built around one prayer, and the people who come for it.

A streak that travels with you

Pray Fajr at any registered mosque and it counts. Away for work or visiting family, you keep your streak and your place at your home mosque.

A podium at every mosque

First, second and third, for everyone or by group: Kids, Youth and Adults, or whatever names the mosque prefers. Ties go to the longest streak, then to whoever arrives earliest.

Prizes the mosque decides

A bicycle for first place among the kids, a medal for 40 days: the admin sets a prize for each place and for reaching a number of days, with the sponsor's name if there is one.

Children without phones

A volunteer ticks names on the attendance list, or scans the printed card each child carries. A parent can also check in the whole family with one scan.

Squads

Up to 10 friends at one mosque who hold each other to Fajr, ranked by days per member. A join code is all it takes.

Mosques competing in good

The world leaderboard ranks approved mosques by total check-ins and by consistency, the share of their people who show up each day, so a small mosque can beat a big one.

First in today

Each morning the first three through the door are named on the mosque's page.

Nine badges

From First Dawn to Rijal al-Fajr, earned at 40 dawns in a row, plus Musafir for praying Fajr at three different mosques.

A card for your story

One tap draws your streak as an image sized for an Instagram story, ready to share.

For mosques and organisations

Run a Fajr challenge without the paper register.

Register your mosque in a few minutes. It works straight away, and joins the world leaderboard once approved.

The mosque screen

A tablet or TV at the entrance shows the code, today's count against your goal and the latest names as they come in.

Or a printed poster

No screen? Print the poster. Its code only works while the phone is at the mosque, checked once by location.

Attendance list

Type a name to find it and tick it. A new name is added and marked in one tap, and correct the last two days if someone was missed.

Member cards

Print a card with a personal QR code for each child. Scan them one after another at the door.

Challenges and prizes

Pick the dates, choose whether Fajr at other mosques counts, and set the prizes. The final podium is kept when it ends.

Numbers you can use

Today's count, a 14-day chart, visitors, and a spreadsheet export of every check-in for the last 90 days.

Admins and volunteers

Volunteers run the screen and the list. Admins also change settings, challenges and prizes.

Organisations

A council, a school network or a city groups its mosques under one leaderboard, with its own challenges and prizes.

Sponsor offers

See who has offered to sponsor your mosque, or any mosque in your country, with their contact details and what they offer.

Practice mode

Try the whole thing at any hour before you launch. The mosque stays off the world leaderboard while it is on.

For sponsors

Put a bicycle on the podium. Or breakfast on the table.

A shop, a family or anyone who wants a share in the reward can offer to sponsor. Leave your details, say how you would like to help, and choose who may contact you.

  • Choose how. Prizes, breakfast after Fajr, money toward prizes, vouchers or products, or something else.
  • Choose who. Any registered mosque or organisation in your country, or one particular mosque you pick.
  • They contact you. Your details are shown only to the admins of those mosques and organisations, never on a public page.

Fair play and privacy

Honest numbers, and very little data.

Only at the mosque, only at Fajr

The screen code expires in under two minutes. The poster needs the phone to be at the mosque. Check-in is closed outside the Fajr window. One check-in per person per day.

Admins can put things right

Staff can remove a wrong check-in, and they are told when several accounts check in from one phone on the same morning.

Made for children's privacy

A child has no account, no email and no password. Only a display name and a group are stored, added by a parent or the mosque.

Yours to hide and yours to delete

Hide your name on the leaderboard with one switch. Delete your account in the app and every check-in of yours goes with it, straight away.

The leaderboard is encouragement, not the goal. The reward for Fajr is with Allah.

Questions

Asked before the first Fajr.

Does it cost anything?

No. It is free for people and for mosques, with no ads.

Do I need to install an app?

No. It runs in your phone's browser, and you can add it to your home screen. Native apps for iPhone and Android are coming soon.

What if I pray Fajr at a different mosque?

It counts. Scan that mosque's code and your streak and your place at your home mosque are kept. The mosque you prayed in gets the check-in on the world leaderboard.

How do children take part?

Three ways: a parent adds them to the family and checks them in with the parent's own scan, a volunteer ticks them on the attendance list, or they show their printed card to be scanned.

Who gives the prizes?

The mosque or organisation that announced them. The app shows the prizes and the standings; it does not sell or hand out anything.

How does sponsoring work?

A sponsor fills in a short form in the app: their details, how they want to help, and whether any mosque in their country or one chosen mosque may contact them. Mosque admins see the offer and get in touch. No money goes through the app.

Can someone check in from home?

The code on the mosque screen changes every 30 seconds and the printed poster checks that the phone is at the mosque. Check-in is also closed outside the Fajr window. Admins can remove a check-in that should not be there.

How is Fajr time worked out?

From the mosque's coordinates and the calculation method its admin chooses, such as Muslim World League, ISNA, Umm al-Qura or Diyanet. The admin sets how long before the adhan check-in opens and when it closes.

How does my mosque get on the world leaderboard?

Register it in the app. It works at once for your own congregation, and appears on the world leaderboard after it has been approved, which keeps fake mosques out.
